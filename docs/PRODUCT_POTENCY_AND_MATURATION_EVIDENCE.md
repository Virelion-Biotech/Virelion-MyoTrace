# Product potency and cardiomyocyte maturation evidence

MyoTrace can contribute functional evidence for cardiomyocyte-derived products,
but marker expression or image motion alone must not be labelled as myocardial
regeneration or clinical potency.

## Separate evidence domains

- Identity: cardiomyocyte identity and contaminating cell populations.
- Purity: fraction of intended cell population and method used.
- Viability: assay, timepoint and handling context.
- Maturity: sarcomere organisation, calcium handling, electrophysiological
  properties, metabolic phenotype and structural organisation.
- Function: paced contraction, force/shortening, calcium transient kinetics and
  response to defined perturbations.
- Safety: spontaneous activity, conduction heterogeneity, arrhythmia-related
  observations and residual proliferative cells where relevant.
- Stability: retention of phenotype and function over time and after storage/
  transport where applicable.

## Reporting requirements

Every measurement should retain units, assay protocol, biological replicate,
technical replicate, cell source, differentiation batch, timepoint, uncertainty,
raw-data provenance and analysis version. Technical replicates must not be counted
as independent biological replicates.

## Interpretation safeguards

- Increased TNNT2, ACTN2 or other marker abundance is not by itself proof of
  functional maturation.
- Increased motion amplitude is not equivalent to increased contractile force.
- In-vitro cardiomyocyte function is not proof of engraftment, electrical coupling
  or myocardial regeneration in vivo.
- Product potency is assay- and mechanism-specific; there is no universal single
  potency assay for all cardiac cell products.

## Interface

MyoTrace reports should be exportable as potency-assay observations with a stable
product/batch identifier and evidence provenance, suitable for attachment to a
CardiAtlas therapeutic-product record. This is an interoperability target; clinical
release criteria remain product- and regulator-specific.
